The Very Hungry Caterpillar by Eric Carle (ages 2-5): A tiny caterpillar eats its way through a week of food, then becomes a beautiful butterfly. Great for counting, days of the week, and bright pictures.
Where the Wild Things Are by Maurice Sendak (ages 3-7): Max is sent to his room, sails to an island of wild monsters, and becomes their king before coming home to a warm supper. A classic about big feelings.
The Gruffalo by Julia Donaldson (ages 3-7): A clever mouse invents a scary monster to frighten off a fox, an owl, and a snake, then meets the real Gruffalo. Rhyming, funny, and fun to read aloud.
Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown (ages 1-4): A little bunny says goodnight to everything in the great green room. A gentle, calming bedtime book.
We're Going on a Bear Hunt by Michael Rosen (ages 2-6): A family splashes through grass, mud, and a river on a bear hunt, then runs all the way home. Full of repetition and sound effects kids love to join in.
Room on the Broom by Julia Donaldson (ages 3-7): A kind witch keeps making room on her broom for new animal friends, who then save her from a hungry dragon. Rhyming and sweet.
The Cat in the Hat by Dr. Seuss (ages 4-8): On a rainy day a mischievous cat turns the house upside down with Thing One and Thing Two. Perfect for beginning readers who like rhymes and silliness.
Green Eggs and Ham by Dr. Seuss (ages 3-7): Sam-I-Am will not give up until his grumpy friend tries green eggs and ham. Simple words, great for kids learning to read.
Charlotte's Web by E. B. White (ages 8-12): A spider named Charlotte uses her web to save Wilbur the pig from the farmer. A moving story about friendship, growing up, and saying goodbye.
Matilda by Roald Dahl (ages 8-12): A brilliant little girl with horrible parents discovers she has special powers and takes on the terrifying headmistress Miss Trunchbull. Funny, clever, and empowering.
Charlie and the Chocolate Factory by Roald Dahl (ages 7-11): Poor Charlie wins a golden ticket to Willy Wonka's mysterious chocolate factory, where greedy children meet sticky ends. Wild imagination and lots of laughs.
Diary of a Wimpy Kid by Jeff Kinney (ages 8-12): Greg Heffley's illustrated diary of surviving middle school, awkward friendships, and an annoying family. Very funny and great for reluctant readers.
Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone by J. K. Rowling (ages 9-13): An orphan discovers he is a wizard and starts at Hogwarts School, where he makes friends and faces a dark secret. Magic, mystery, and adventure.
The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe by C. S. Lewis (ages 8-12): Four children step through a wardrobe into the snowy land of Narnia and help the lion Aslan defeat the White Witch. A classic fantasy adventure.
Wonder by R. J. Palacio (ages 9-13): Auggie, a boy born with a facial difference, starts school for the first time and learns who his real friends are. A kind, heartfelt story about acceptance.
Dog Man by Dav Pilkey (ages 6-10): A half-dog, half-police-officer hero fights crime and the evil cat Petey in a hilarious comic-book series. Perfect for kids who love graphic novels and jokes.
The Magic Faraway Tree by Enid Blyton (ages 6-10): Three children climb an enormous tree where a new magical land appears at the top every week. Cosy, old-fashioned adventure with lots of imagination.
Percy Jackson and the Lightning Thief by Rick Riordan (ages 9-13): Percy discovers he is the son of a Greek god and must find Zeus's stolen lightning bolt. Fast, funny, and full of mythology.
Hairy Maclary from Donaldson's Dairy by Lynley Dodd (ages 2-6): A scruffy little dog and his dog friends set off on a walk until they meet Scarface Claw, the toughest cat in town. Bouncy rhymes and lovable characters.
Possum Magic by Mem Fox (ages 3-7): Grandma Poss makes Hush invisible, and they travel around Australia eating classic foods to make her visible again. A beloved Australian picture book.
